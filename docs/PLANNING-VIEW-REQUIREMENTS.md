## 2. The flow

This replaces today's six steps (Orient, Do it as a student, Predict, Rehearse, Adjust, Carry it). The order is the one Josh proposed and Robert restated (2:12:56, 2:14:02). It follows the three IPP sections: Understand the End Goal, Lesson Activities, Lesson Synthesis.

### Step 1: Understand the end goal (IPP: Lesson-Level, about 5 min)

- **R1.1** Show the lesson's place in the unit's story and how it builds on the previous lesson, drawing on IM's lesson narrative, learning goals, lesson synthesis and student summary. Keep it short and fold the rest.
- **R1.2** Show the **essential understanding** (one math idea) as the destination.
- **R1.3** Show the **progression of activities**: the arc, chunked by activity, with each activity's job and the crux marked. Keep "tap for IM's notes."
- **R1.4** Name the math practice standards an activity advances, where IM tags them, in one line. *(IPP item; low priority.)*

### Step 2: The language goal, or CLO (IPP: Unpack the CLO, about 5 min)

This is the biggest gap. Robert (1:16:20) called it "a glaring hole," and "it is an expectation, so therefore, it needs to be in the tool."

- **R2.1** Show **one CLO** for the lesson. It starts from IM's learning goal. IM's middle-school goals carry the mode ("orally and in writing") but not the **form and function** DPS requires (Heather 1:07:34), so Premo adds:
  - **Function:** the language use (explain, justify, compare…).
  - **Form:** the sentence construction, for example "rate per 1 in a reasonable sentence."
  - **Key terms:** which must be precise and which can be informal (Robert 0:55:44).
  
  Teachers do not write their own CLO.
- **R2.2** **"Sounds like" exemplar.** The teacher first says or writes what a strong student sentence sounds like, then sees Premo's exemplar. Josh: take "a swing" at it, then "here's yours rounded up" (2:12:56). IM's CLOs are vague enough that three experts would write different exemplars (Josh 2:18:38), so the tool must say what it should sound like.
- **R2.3** **Vocabulary in use, not a glossary.** Show the key terms in the exemplar sentence. "Vocabulary plus definition is not it" (Josh 2:12:05). The words come from DSST's Unit at a Glance where it exists (IPP), otherwise from IM's unit terminology.
- **R2.4** The CLO and exemplar must carry through to the crux, the coach output and the posted exemplar (R6.2).

### Step 3: Do the cool-down (IPP: Solve the cool-down with exemplar work)

- **R3.1** The teacher solves IM's cool-down, including a written explanation, then compares it with IM's response and the look-fors. This works as today.
- **R3.2** The look-fors include the CLO: does the explanation use the form and terms from Step 2?
- **R3.3** Remove the 20-character minimum that gates "Check my answer" (0:45:04). Any non-empty answer is enough.
- **R3.4** Experienced teachers start here (Josh 1:52:20). Steps 1–3 can be done in any order, and the tool doesn't lock them.

### Step 4: Choose activities (new)

- **R4.1** The crux is preselected and can't be removed. The label reads "Required" (DSST: "do not pass go").
- **R4.2** The teacher may add any other activity. The warm-up is never required (Josh 2:15:16).
- **R4.3** The Rehearse step contains only the chosen activities, in lesson order.

### Step 5: Rehearse each chosen activity (IPP: Lesson Activities, about 10–15 min)

Each activity is worked through on its own before the next one starts (P5). Every chosen activity gets parts **a–d**. The crux also gets the language layer, **e–g** (Josh's red items). Non-crux activities carry little or no language support (Josh 2:16:45).

- **a. Do the math.** The exemplar response for the activity's task, with strategies and representations. Anticipate more than one correct strategy, not just right and wrong (Heather 0:28:43). For a Five Practices activity, include the order to share in.
- **b. Barriers and misconceptions.**
  - *Barriers* block access to the CLO, such as vocabulary or prior knowledge.
  - *Misconceptions* arise on the way to the understanding (Heather 1:51:12).
  
  Both come only from this activity. At the crux, add the **language errors tied to the CLO** (Josh 2:00:50).
- **c. Plan the response.** Back-pocket questions, one-on-one feedback and whole-class redirects. Today's say-aloud rehearsal with criteria stays (Predict, then say your move, then check).
- **d. Grouping and monitoring.** How students work (alone, pairs, groups) and what to monitor and track during the activity.
- **e. Launch the crux with Three Reads (MLR 6).** Lesson-specific sample language for each read (Josh 1:01:00, 2:08:05).
- **f. Release with a frame (MLR 8, always on).**
  - Name the **language success criteria** as students are released, for example "be sure you're naming…" (Josh 2:06:17).
  - Give the sentence frame, differentiated by the WIDA levels in the class. This uses the existing level picker and tier rule.
  - Name one or two moments of student talk practice, such as a turn-and-talk (Robert 1:02:40).
- **g. Synthesize with MLR 1 or MLR 2.**
  - Sample language for Stronger and Clearer Each Time or Collect and Display in the activity synthesis.
  - Plan for students to share their reasoning in CLO language at least once (Josh 2:01:20).
  - The synthesis is where the CLO's verb gets met (Heather 1:16:23).

**MLRs come first in the activity, never at the bottom** (Heather 0:58:11). Each step shows its routine at the point it happens: launch, release or synthesis.

### Step 6: Lesson synthesis (IPP: Lesson Synthesis, about 5 min)

- **R6.1** Show IM's lesson synthesis questions, with desired and anticipated responses and one back-pocket question.
- *Open:* Josh asks whether language guidance belongs here too. See D5.

### Step 7: Carry it (output)

- **R7.1** **In-class card.** The crux's Three Reads lines, the frame by level, the language success criteria and the synthesis prompt. These are the lines the teacher rehearsed.
- **R7.2** **Posted exemplar.** A printable or shareable "what it sounds like" for the room (CLO, exemplar sentence, frame), so the tool covers the posting expectation with no double work (Heather 1:04:35).
- **R7.3** **Coach summary** with the coach's **look-fors** (Josh 2:01:20–2:02:49; Heather 2:17:46):
  - the CLO and its exemplar;
  - the MLR done well;
  - stems in use;
  - the teacher monitoring language;
  - students producing CLO language at least once in the synthesis.
  
  Most coaches are new or not math specialists, so the summary says what each look-for looks and sounds like.
- **R7.4** Keep "If you run short" (cuts), never-cuts and the before-class checklist. DSST liked them (transcript, section 9).

## 3. Other requirements

- **R8.1 Content only from this lesson.** Fix the bug where responses came from another activity (0:46–0:48). In `unit3-rates`, the checker enforces that moments reference the lesson's own activities. Extend it so each rehearsed moment sits in a chosen activity.
- **R8.2 Rehearsal recording** (Heather asked, 0:46:39). The option is to record the spoken rehearsal on the device for the teacher's own playback, not uploaded. *Needs a decision (D6).*
- **R8.3 Group use.** It works for a two-teacher grade team in a 30-minute LICM (Heather 2:21:53), and for a coach narrowing it to the crux for 15 minutes of practice (Josh 1:59:18).
- **R8.4 Time.** The whole flow, with only the crux chosen, fits in 20–30 minutes. The step estimates on screen must add up to that.

- **R8.5 Saved planning record.** Teachers must keep a lesson planning folder and share it with the Pilot Manager. The tool saves each night's prep (answers, CLO exemplar, chosen activities, flags) in a form that can go into that folder: a PDF, a copyable doc, or a link. *(Pilot R&R)*
- **R8.6 Grades 6–8.** The pilot is 6th–8th grade math. Content must exist for the lessons pilot teachers actually teach from Jan 5, not only Grade 6 Unit 3. *(Pilot R&R; see D8)*
- **R8.7 Coach preparation.** DCIs use the tool to prepare for and lead biweekly LICMs, and one LICM per teacher per phase is filmed. The coach summary (R7.3) and the crux-only path (R8.3) must be usable by a DCI on a teacher's lesson. *(Pilot R&R)*
- **R8.8 Quality review.** The pilot runs a monthly "Quality of Supports Review." Each piece of Premo guidance keeps its source (IM, Premo, or DSST) so reviewers can tell which is which. *(Pilot R&R)*

**From DSST's pilot roles document** ("Premo Pilot Schools R&R's," Heather Patel, last edited Oct 28):
- **Timeline:** onboarding at PLC #1 in December; Phase 1 from Jan 5 to Feb 17; Phase 2 from Mar 1 to May 21.
- **Teachers:** they use Premo daily during internalization, "integrating the tool's guidance on language demands, Math Language Routines (MLRs), WIDA-aligned language objectives, and learner-specific scaffolds."
- **Observations:** DCI observations (about four per classroom, filmed in Sibme) look for "Premo tool integration, student talk ratios, and implementation of Math Language Routines." The coach look-fors in R7.3 should line up with them.
- **Comparison materials:** teachers submit lesson planning materials made without Premo, before and after the pilot. The saved record (R8.5) should be comparable with those.

## 4. What changes from the current `unit3-rates` build

| Today | Next version |
|---|---|
| Six steps; Orient first | Seven IPP-named steps; end goal, then CLO, then cool-down |
| Language goal is one line in Orient | Its own step, with CLO form and function plus a "sounds like" exemplar the teacher drafts first |
| Predict the crux is a separate step | Folded into each activity's part b (anticipate) |
| Six rehearsed moments chosen by the author, across activities | The teacher chooses activities; the crux is required; each activity is rehearsed on its own |
| The crux's language routine follows IM's own support (e.g. Compare and Connect in 6.3.7) | DSST's priority routines at the crux (D1): Three Reads launch, MLR 8 frame, MLR 1 or 2 synthesis |
| Adjust holds the language supports | Language moves into the crux rehearsal (e–g); Adjust keeps only the cuts |
| Coach summary lists flags and answers | Adds the CLO, the exemplar and five look-fors; adds a posted exemplar |
| 20-character minimum on answers | Removed |

Unchanged: IM's text verbatim with live notes, the cool-down, the crux callout, cuts and never-cuts, the before-class checklist, the WIDA level picker and tier rule, and the In-class card format.
