# Rehearsal guidance rule

**Approved by Robert on 2026-11-03, with one change:** "Where they get stuck" also appears on the in-class card as **Watch for**. It lists the crux's misconceptions and barriers, including the stuck points no card rehearses. On the Do the task cards it stays folded. The rule is built and checked: `scripts/u3-check.mjs` requires each card type's guidance.

This rule covers the guidance at the bottom of each rehearsal card. It sets which kinds of guidance a card gets, decided by the kind of card rather than case by case, so all five lessons come out the same way. Once approved, the content checker requires each kind wherever it applies.

## Moves (Robert, 2026-11-03)

- **What counts as a move:** each card is one move, except Do the task cards, which are solving, not saying. Three Reads (three rounds) and the by-level card (one round per WIDA band picked) each count as **one move**.
- **Crux-only night:** a target of **8 moves**. The checker warns above 8 and fails above 10.
- **Each added activity:** at most **2 moves**. Choose activities shows the moves and minutes each activity adds.
- **The counter** on Rehearse counts moves, with an estimate of about 3 minutes per task and 1.5 per move.
- **Not on screen:** a source label for new student work. Its source stays in each lesson's notes.

**Wording:** "Where we are" is followed by a period. Aims are 15 words or fewer; other lines 25.

## Card types

Every rehearsal card is one of these six types.

| Type | What it rehearses | 6.3.7 examples |
|---|---|---|
| **Do the task** | Solving the activity's task the way a strong student would | Do the task yourself |
| **Routine** | Running a routine: the launch or the synthesis | Read the problem three times · Finish on the board |
| **Send to work** | Releasing students with the language they must use | Send students to work |
| **Student moment** | Responding to one student's thinking or error | A student is stuck · A student writes "14 per 2" |
| **Language moment** | Helping a student say the idea in the language goal's words | Help a student say it in words |
| **Decision** | A choice the teacher makes, such as share order or a class prompt | Choose who shares first · Hands up too early |

## Kinds of guidance

| # | Guidance | What it is | Limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Model** | Words the teacher could say | One or two lines |
| 2 | **Sounds like** | A strong student answer in the language goal's form | One sentence; uses the precise terms |
| 3 | **By WIDA level** | For each level picked: the frame, a way in without words, and the home-language line, under the level-tier rule | Fixed format |
| 4 | **Did yours do these?** | Three checks, past tense, never marked wrong | Exactly 3 |
| 5 | **What's going on** | Why the student did that, and what they can already do | Two sentences at most; asset-framed |
| 6 | **Avoid** | The tempting move that takes the thinking away from the student | One line |
| 7 | **If it doesn't work** | The next question if the first move doesn't land | One question; must not give the answer |
| 8 | **Why** | The purpose of this routine or choice, here | Two sentences at most; IM's reason where IM gives one |
| 9 | **Run it** | The routine's steps for this activity | 3–5 steps |
| 10 | **Where they get stuck / Ask if they're stuck** | IM's misconceptions and the barriers, plus 2–3 back-pocket questions | As now |

## Which card types get which guidance

✓ = required · ○ = optional · — = never

| Guidance | Do the task | Routine | Send to work | Student moment | Language moment | Decision |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Model | ✓ (IM's answer) | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ (by level) | ✓ |
| Sounds like | — | ✓ | ✓ | ○ | ✓ | — |
| By WIDA level | — | — | ✓ | — | ✓ | — |
| Did yours do these? | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| What's going on | — | — | — | ✓ | ✓ | ○ |
| Avoid | — | ○ | ○ | ✓ | ✓ | ✓ |
| If it doesn't work | — | ○ | ○ | ✓ | ✓ | — |
| Why | — | ✓ | ✓ | — | — | ✓ |
| Run it | — | ✓ | — | — | — | — |
| Where they get stuck / Ask if stuck | ✓ | — | — | — | — | — |

**Reasons, in brief**
- **Student and language moments** always get What's going on, Avoid and If it doesn't work. These are the cards where a first move most often misses, and where the tempting fix, saying it for them, does the most harm.
- **Routine, send-to-work and decision cards** always get **Why**. A teacher who knows the routine's purpose can adapt it when the class doesn't follow the script.
- **Anything about language** always gets **Sounds like**. The teacher needs the target in front of them, not in memory.

## Where guidance comes from, in this order

1. **IM's teacher materials.** IM's answers, anticipated misconceptions, activity synthesis, English-learner supports and routine definitions. Where IM gives the answer or the reason, the guidance uses IM's words or stays close to them.
2. **DSST's direction:**

   - DSST's WIDA guidance, through the level-tier rule.
3. **Premo's judgment,** only where IM and DSST are silent: the moments, Avoid, If it doesn't work, Why when IM gives no reason, and new student work. Premo's guidance stays marked draft until a person reviews it, and new student work is labeled NEW.

## Writing rules for guidance

1. **Plain words** a tired teacher can read at 9 pm. One idea per line; no line over 25 words.
2. **Never give the student the answer,** in Model or in If it doesn't work.
3. **Asset framing:** What's going on starts from what the student can already do.
4. **Same order on every card.**
   - **Shown:** Model, Sounds like, By WIDA level, Did yours do these?, Avoid.
   - **Folded:** What's going on, If it doesn't work, Why, Run it, Where they get stuck.
5. **One name for each kind,** exactly as in the table above, on every card.
